Objective

The goal of Chinese Chess is to Checkmate (capture) the enemy General. You win if the enemy General is under attack and has no legal moves to escape. Additionally, causing a Stalemate (where the opponent has no legal moves even if not in check) counts as a win for the player who made the last move.

Board Geography

The River (楚河 漢界)

The central division representing the historical border between the Chu and Han states. It acts as a physical barrier: Elephants (相/象) are forbidden from crossing it. Additionally, Soldiers (兵/卒) undergo a "promotion" upon crossing, gaining the ability to move sideways for the remainder of the game.

The Palace (九宮)

A 3x3 grid marked with diagonal lines where the General (帥/將) and Advisors (仕/士) reside. These pieces are royalty and are strictly confined to this area; they cannot step outside its boundaries under any circumstances. The diagonal lines also define the movement paths for the Advisors.

Horse's Leg (別馬腿)

A tactical blocking rule. The Horse moves one step orthogonally and then one step diagonally. If a piece (friend or foe) occupies the point directly adjacent to the Horse in its initial straight step, the Horse's path is "blocked" and it cannot complete the move in that direction.

Elephant's Eye (塞象眼)

Similar to the Horse's Leg, the Elephant moves in a 2x2 diagonal leap. If any piece occupies the exact center of that 2x2 square (the "eye"), the Elephant is blocked from jumping to its destination point. This is a common defensive tactic to trap an opponent's Elephant.
